        * BWF ships no name for its pre-2018 categories, so results from the Superseries era are placed on the tier they became, traced through the tournaments themselves: Superseries Premier as Super 1000, Superseries as Super 750, Grand Prix Gold as Super 300 and Grand Prix as Super 100. Those squares carry a notch out of one corner, and hovering one says what it actually was. The Seasons page never translates — there a Superseries Premier is called a Superseries Premier. Four old tiers become five new ones, so nothing lands on Super 500; and before 2011 there was no Premier tier at all, which makes a 2008 All England a rung lower than a 2013 one.

        Every result that reached the chosen round, whatever season it was in — one row per level, hardest at the top. Each row has φ times the area of the row below, so an Olympic square covers about thirty Super 100 ones. The Continentals sit just above the Super 1000s and share their size — an Asian Championships is a major — which leaves the five Supers an unbroken run, stepping evenly all the way down. An empty row is a claim too: hover it to see whether they never got that far or never entered. Comparing two players mirrors the board about the centre line, so the best results of each meet in the middle.

        One column per court, and down each one the matches in the order they are played — so two cards on the same row are at the same point in the day. The y-axis is the running order, not the clock: BWF publishes a flat 50-minute estimate per match and on some courts they run backwards, so only the first match on a court has a real time. The rest are marked ≈ and carry BWF's own Followed by. Click a match to star it — starred matches stay lit and the rest stay dimmed, so a handful of them read at a glance across a whole day. While a tournament is on, the scores are checked every minute and anything that moved is marked for a few minutes after.
